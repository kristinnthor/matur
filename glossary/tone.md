# Tone and structure

## Voice

- **Imperative plural** throughout: *Steikið*, *Bætið*, *Hrærið*, *Látið standa*.
  Never *þú* or infinitives.
- Plain and practical. No food-blog throat-clearing, no "this is the BEST ever".
- Explain *why* when it changes the outcome: *"ekki troða í pottinn, annars sýður kjötið
  í stað þess að brúnast"*. One clause, not a paragraph.
- Icelandic quotation and punctuation: use the en dash `–` for ranges (`2–3 mínútur`), and
  a decimal comma, never a decimal point.

## Steps

- Reference ingredients as `{{id}}` so quantities inline and stay correct when scaled.
  Never write the number into step prose.
- One action per step where practical. A step should be something you can finish before
  looking back at the screen.
- Front-load the verb.

## Notes

Three optional fields, each with a distinct job:

- **`improvements`** — what was changed from the source, and why. Always start
  *"Endurbætur frá upprunalegu uppskriftinni:"*. Be specific and factual. If a source's
  quantities were implausible, say so and give the numbers:
  > *magntölurnar í frumuppskriftinni ganga ekki upp. Þar er talað um 25 g majónes … á móti
  > heilu kílói af kartöflum*

  If nothing was changed, omit the field. Do not invent improvements to fill it.
- **`storage`** — fridge days, freezer months, reheating cautions.
- **`variants`** — substitutions, dietary alternatives, availability notes.

## What counts as an improvement

Real ones, drawn from the five done by hand:

- Fixing implausible quantities (kartöflusalat: 25 g mayo per kg of potatoes)
- Fixing an unrealistic stated time (stroganoff: 10 min for two batches plus vegetables)
- Adding a technique that prevents a known failure (tempering sour cream so it does not split)
- Correcting advice that discards flavour (lamb: scraping *all* the rub off before grilling)
- Supplying missing servings, times, or resting periods
- Adding a method for equipment an Icelandic cook actually has (pan instead of grill)

Not improvements: restyling prose, adding ingredients for novelty, or changing a dish into
something else.
